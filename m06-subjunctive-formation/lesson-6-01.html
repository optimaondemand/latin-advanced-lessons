📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every verb you have met for two years has been making a claim about the world. Today Latin stops claiming.
Cicerō dīcit — "Cicero speaks." That is a fact, and Latin has a whole system for facts: the indicative mood, all six tenses of it, active and passive. You own that system. Today you get a second one, built for what is not fact — what might be, what someone wishes were so, what nobody is sure about. It is called the subjunctive, and it is the largest single addition of this course.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what a mood is, and say what the subjunctive does that the indicative cannot.
2
I will be able to form the present active subjunctive of all four conjugations, all six persons.
3
I will be able to tell amās from amēs — and say which one is a fact.
📚 PART 1 · WHAT A MOOD IS

A verb's tense tells you when. A verb's voice tells you whether the subject acts or is acted on. A verb's mood tells you something different from either: what kind of claim the speaker is making.

INDICATIVE — THE MOOD OF FACT
Catilīna ex urbe discēdit.
"Catiline is leaving the city." This happened. I am telling you it happened.
SUBJUNCTIVE — THE MOOD OF NOT-QUITE
Catilīna ex urbe discēdat.
"May Catiline leave the city." He has not. I am putting the idea in the air.

One letter apart. Two completely different acts of speech. The subjunctive is the mood Latin uses for:

It carriesWhich sounds like this in English
Possibility"he might come," "this could happen"
Wish"may he come," "if only he would"
Doubt"I am not sure whether he is coming"
Restraint"one ought not," "let us not"
Subordinate ideas"so that he might hear," "because he was afraid," "he asked why they were silent"
💡 English still has one, barely. "I wish he were here" — not was. "If I were you" — not am. "The court insists that he appear" — not appears. Those odd-sounding verbs are the last survivors of an English subjunctive. Latin's is not a survivor. It is alive, regular, and everywhere.
📜 Where the name comes from. Roman grammarians called it the modus subiūnctīvus — from sub ("underneath") + iungō ("I join"). The joined-underneath mood: the mood of the clause that hangs under another clause. That name is a promise about where you will mostly meet it, and Modules 8 through 12 are that promise being kept. This module is about the forms themselves.

📚 PART 2 · THE PRESENT SUBJUNCTIVE: ONE VOWEL DOES ALL THE WORK

Here is the whole rule, and it is shorter than you are expecting. The present subjunctive keeps the personal endings you already know — -m/-ō, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt — and changes the vowel in front of them.

1st conjugation: the stem vowel -ā- is replaced by -ē-.
Everything else: -ā- is added to the present stem.

First conjugation, then. Amō, amāre gives the stem am-, and the subjunctive vowel is ē:

1st conj. — amō, amāreSingularPlural
1st personamemamēmus
2nd personamēsamētis
3rd personametament
✍️ Why some of those e's are short. A long vowel shortens before final -m, -t and -nt — which is why you write amem, amet, ament but amēs, amēmus, amētis. You have already lived with this: it is the same reason the indicative is amat and not amāt. Learn the pattern, not a list.

Now the other four. Same idea, different vowel: take the present stem and put -ā- in front of the endings. For 3rd io-stem and 4th verbs the stem already ends in -i-, so you get -iā-.

ConjugationStemPresent active subjunctive
2nd — moneō, monēremone-moneam, moneās, moneat, moneāmus, moneātis, moneant
3rd — regō, regerereg-regam, regās, regat, regāmus, regātis, regant
3rd io-stem — capiō, caperecapi-capiam, capiās, capiat, capiāmus, capiātis, capiant
4th — audiō, audīreaudi-audiam, audiās, audiat, audiāmus, audiātis, audiant
MEMORY WORK · SAY IT OUT LOUD
amem · moneam · regam · capiam · audiam
Five first-person forms, one per conjugation, in order. Learn that chant and you have the vowel of every present subjunctive in Latin: e once, then a four times. One oddity out of five is a good deal.
⚠️ PART 3 · THE MACRON THAT CHANGES THE MOOD

In the first conjugation, the subjunctive vowel ē lands exactly where the indicative had ā. That means the difference between "you love" and "may you love" is one letter, and in some persons only one macron. This is not a trick question the course invented; it is how Latin works, and Roman readers handled it by paying attention.

Indicative — the factSubjunctive — not the factWhat changed
amās — "you love"amēs — "you may love"the stem vowel: ā → ē
amat — "he loves"amet — "he may love"one letter, both short
amāmus — "we love"amēmus — "we may love"the stem vowel again
monet — "he warns"moneat — "he may warn"an a appears out of nowhere
regit — "he rules"regat — "he may rule"i becomes a
audit — "he hears"audiat — "he may hear"an a is added after the i
⚠️ The mistake to head off right now. A long ē is not a subjunctive signal by itself. Monēs ("you warn") and manēs ("you remain") are plain present indicatives — that ē is simply the 2nd-conjugation stem vowel. The subjunctive of those verbs is moneās and maneās, with the added a. Before you call a form subjunctive, ask which conjugation the verb belongs to. The vowel only means something once you know that.

★ TEMPERANTIA · SELF-GOVERNMENT

Temperantia is built on temperāre, "to mix in the right proportion" — from tempus, the same root that gives Latin tempestās, weather. Unmixed weather is a storm. A person of temperantia is not timid; they are proportioned. They know how much is enough, and they can stop.

The subjunctive is the grammar of that. The indicative declares: this is so. The subjunctive weighs: this might be so, this ought to be so, I am not certain it is so. Cicero in 63 BC could have arrested Catiline in the middle of his own speech and did not — and the whole first Catilīnarian is an argument in the subjunctive mood about why. Learning these endings is learning how a language says hold on. That is a small piece of self-government, done with verbs.

The same vowel, the passive endings you already own: amer, amēris, amētur. Then the deponents collect their reward — loquar, loquāris, loquātur — and you meet the only three present subjunctives in Latin worth memorising as a list: sim, possim, eam.
💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

Mood is not tense. The subjunctive is a second way of holding a verb — the mood of the possible, the wished-for, and the doubted. To build the present subjunctive: 1st conjugation swaps ā for ē; every other conjugation adds ā. Chant it: amem, moneam, regam, capiam, audiam.
