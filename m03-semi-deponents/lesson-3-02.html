📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
One verb, six tenses, zero new endings.
Today is a paradigm day, and it is an easy one, because every ending on this page is an ending you already own. The present half uses ordinary 2nd-conjugation active endings, like videō or timeō. The perfect half uses participle plus esse, like locūtus sum. Your job is to keep them from bleeding into each other.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to produce the present, imperfect, and future of audeō in all six persons.
2
I will be able to build the perfect, pluperfect, and future perfect of audeō with the participle agreeing correctly.
3
I will be able to spot and reject the two forms students invent most: *auduī and *audeor.
📚 PART 1 · ABOVE THE SEAM — THE PRESENT SYSTEM

Audeō is 2nd conjugation, stem aud-ē-. Conjugate it exactly as you would timeō or videō: ordinary active endings -ō, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt.

Present — "I dare"

SingularPlural
1staudeō — I dareaudēmus — we dare
2ndaudēs — you dareaudētis — you (pl.) dare
3rdaudet — he/she daresaudent — they dare

Imperfect — the -bā- marker

SingularPlural
1staudēbam — I was daringaudēbāmus — we were daring
2ndaudēbās — you were daringaudēbātis — you (pl.) were daring
3rdaudēbat — he/she was daringaudēbant — they were daring

Future — 2nd conjugation, so -bi-

SingularPlural
1staudēbō — I will dareaudēbimus — we will dare
2ndaudēbis — you will dareaudēbitis — you (pl.) will dare
3rdaudēbit — he/she will dareaudēbunt — they will dare
🎵 MEMORY WORK · THE ACTIVE HALF · SAY IT ALOUD THREE TIMES
audeō, audēs, audet —
audēmus, audētis, audent
💡 Nothing here is special. Set audeō beside videō, vidēre, vīdī, vīsum and the present systems are identical: videt / audet, vidēbat / audēbat, vidēbit / audēbit. The two verbs part company only when you reach the perfect — vīdit versus ausus est.
📚 PART 2 · BELOW THE SEAM — THE PERFECT SYSTEM

Now switch modes. The perfect system of audeō is built the way every deponent perfect is built: the participle ausus, -a, -um plus a form of esse. Which tense of esse you use decides which tense you get.

TenseForm (3rd sg. masculine)Meaning
Perfectausus esthe dared, he has dared
Pluperfectausus erathe had dared
Future perfectausus erithe will have dared

Full perfect of audeō

SingularPlural
1stausus / ausa sum — I daredausī / ausae sumus — we dared
2ndausus / ausa es — you daredausī / ausae estis — you (pl.) dared
3rdausus / ausa / ausum est — he/she/it daredausī / ausae / ausa sunt — they dared
🎵 MEMORY WORK · THE DEPONENT HALF · SAY IT ALOUD THREE TIMES
ausus sum, ausus es, ausus est —
ausī sumus, ausī estis, ausī sunt
✍️ The participle agrees, exactly as it did last week. Ausus is an adjective and it matches its subject in gender and number. A man says ausus sum; a woman says ausa sum; a group of women says ausae sumus. In the reading on Friday, one form of this verb has a feminine participle, and that participle is how you know who is doing the daring.
💡 And the infinitives split the same way. Present audēre, "to dare" — an ordinary active infinitive. Perfect ausus esse, "to have dared" — participle plus esse, like a deponent. Future ausūrus esse, "to be about to dare." You will need the perfect infinitive whenever audeō turns up inside an indirect statement.
⚠️ PART 3 · THE TWO FORMS THAT DO NOT EXIST

Almost every mistake in this module is one of two errors. Both come from carrying a rule across the seam, in one direction or the other.

ERROR 1 · ACTIVE PERFECT ENDINGS
*auduī  *audēvī  *ausit
You saw an active present and assumed an active perfect. But audeō has no one-word perfect at all. Correct: ausus sum, ausus est.
ERROR 2 · PASSIVE PRESENT ENDINGS
*audeor  *audētur  *audēbātur
You heard the word "deponent" and reached for -or / -tur. But the present system is active in form. Correct: audeō, audet, audēbat.
⛔ One sentence to keep. A full deponent is passive-looking everywhere; a semi-deponent is passive-looking only in the perfect system. So loquitur is right and *loquit is wrong — but audet is right and *audētur is wrong. The two verb types are opposite in the present and identical in the perfect.

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

Above the seam, audeō is videō: audet, audēbat, audēbit. Below the seam, audeō is loquor: ausus est, ausus erat, ausus erit. There is no *auduī and no *audētur — each of those tries to make one half behave like the other.
