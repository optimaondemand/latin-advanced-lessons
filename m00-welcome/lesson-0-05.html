📚 PART 1 · MODULE 0 ON ONE PAGE
Nouns · from 0.02
Genitive singular names the family: -ae · -ī · -is · -ūs · -eī. Then the case ending names the job: subject, of, to/for, object, by/with/from/in.
Verbs · from 0.03
Stem, marker, ending. Present stem carries present / imperfect / future in both voices. Perfect stem carries the perfect active tenses. PPP plus esse carries the perfect passives.
Syntax · from 0.04
Ablative absolute sets circumstances. Accusative + infinitive reports speech and thought. Quī/quae/quod takes gender and number from above, case from its own clause.
✍️ What Module 1 does with this. Next week takes the same material and stops giving you tables. You will retrieve these forms out of continuous adapted Sallust instead — the way you will need them for the rest of the year.
📜 PART 2 · ONE REAL SENTENCE OF CICERO

On 8 November 63 BC, Cicero stood up in a temple full of senators. Catiline — who had already tried to have him killed — was sitting in the room. Cicero opened like this. These are Cicero's own words, unadapted: one sentence, fully glossed, so you can see the real thing.

Quō ūsque tandem abūtēre, Catilīna, patientiā nostrā?
Cicero, In Catilīnam I.1 (unadapted)
WordWhat it is
quō ūsque"to what point," i.e. how long — a set phrase
tandem"at last, finally" — here it adds impatience, like English "just how long"
abūtēre2nd person singular future of abūtor, "to misuse, abuse" — "will you go on abusing." A deponent verb — passive in form, active in meaning. That is Module 2.
CatilīnaVocative — Cicero names him to his face, in front of the Senate
patientiā nostrāablative — abūtor takes its object in the ablative, not the accusative: "our patience"
"How long, Catiline, will you go on abusing our patience?"
💡 Notice what one sentence just cost you. Two things stood in your way: a deponent verb, and a verb that governs the ablative instead of the accusative. Neither is exotic — both are ordinary Latin, and both arrive in Modules 2 and 3. This is exactly why the year is built the way it is: not to make you memorize more, but to remove the specific things that currently stop you reading Romans directly.
