📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Not hundreds of verb forms — two stems and a short list of markers.
You know six tenses in two voices across four conjugations. Written out, that is a wall of forms. Organized properly, it is two stems, each with its own small set of markers. Get the organization back today, because next year's subjunctive is built on exactly these stems.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to pull the present stem, the perfect stem, and the participial stem out of a verb's four principal parts.
2
I will be able to name the tense, voice, person, and number of any indicative form I meet.
3
I will be able to tell the three participles apart and say when each one happens.
📚 PART 1 · FOUR PRINCIPAL PARTS, THREE STEMS

A dictionary entry hands you everything you need, if you know what each part is for:

laudō, laudāre, laudāvī, laudātum — to praise
PartGives youUsed to build
1st — laudōthe dictionary form"I praise" — and it tells you the conjugation together with part 2
2nd — laudārepresent stem laudā-present, imperfect, future — active and passive (the "present system")
3rd — laudāvīperfect stem laudāv-perfect, pluperfect, future perfect — active only
4th — laudātumparticipial stem laudāt-the PPP, and therefore every perfect passive tense; also the FAP
💡 Why this matters for this year: the subjunctive you meet in Module 6 is not a new set of forms bolted on. The present subjunctive is built on the present stem; the imperfect subjunctive on the present infinitive; the perfect and pluperfect subjunctive on the perfect stem and the PPP. Every stem you refresh today gets reused.

TenseMarkerExample (laudō, 3rd sg.)
Presentnone — stem + endinglaudat (he praises) · laudātur (he is praised)
Imperfect-bā-laudābat (he was praising) · laudābātur (he was being praised)
Future — 1st & 2nd conj.-bi-laudābit (he will praise) · laudābitur (he will be praised)
Future — 3rd & 4th conj.-a- / -ē-reget (he will rule) · regētur (he will be ruled)
✍️ The trap worth remembering. In the 3rd and 4th conjugations, present and future look dangerously alike: regit is "he rules," reget is "he will rule." One vowel. Read carefully — and expect Latin to do this to you again in Module 2, where loqueris ("you speak") and loquēris ("you will speak") differ only by a macron.
📚 PART 3 · THE PERFECT SYSTEM

Three more tenses — but here active and passive are built completely differently. Active uses the perfect stem. Passive uses the PPP plus a form of esse.

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

Every Latin verb form answers three questions: which stem is it built on, which marker sits after the stem, and which ending closes it. Stem, marker, ending. That is the whole indicative system — and it is the frame the subjunctive will drop into.
